What Super Electric does

From one switch to a full service upgrade

Available for all your electrical needs. Residential and commercial; renovations and new construction; the small jobs and the big ones.

Panels

Service upgrades

More capacity, a modern breaker panel and a clean, labelled install. A service upgrade is what makes room for the loads older homes were never wired for.

Home · Business

Wiring & rewiring

New circuits, added receptacles and complete rewires — copper run cleanly, protected properly and terminated tight.

Older homes

Knob & tube removal

Pre-1950s wiring with no ground and brittle insulation. Removing it and rewiring is a safety upgrade — and many insurers ask for it before they will cover the house.

Home

Basement development

The electrical side of a finished basement, from rough-in to the last cover plate: circuits, lighting and receptacles laid out for how the space will actually be used.

Home · Business

In general terms: the panel is replaced with a larger one (100 amps to 200 is the common jump), and the meter base, mast and service conductors are usually renewed at the same time. The utility disconnects and reconnects power, the work is permitted and inspected, and the existing branch circuits are moved onto the new breakers. See Fig. 01 above for the parts involved.

Knob and tube has no ground, its insulation is often brittle after seventy-plus years, and it was never meant to sit under modern attic insulation. Many insurers will not cover a home until it is removed. Removal and rewiring is listed as one of Super Electric Service’s services — call and describe the house.

Before this goes live: things to confirm with the owner
  • Old website. A domain, superelectricservice.ca, still shows up in search results but no longer resolves and has no archived copy. This draft assumes there is no live site. Is the domain still yours? It would be the natural home for this one.
  • Address. YellowPages lists a suite address in Beddington; BBB and Kijiji give only the T3P postal area with the street kept private; Yelp shows no address. No address is published on this page — the site shows the service area only. Say if you want one shown.
  • Email. None is published anywhere. The contact card and footer have a slot waiting for one.
  • Hours. The only source is an unclaimed Yelp listing (8 AM to 7 PM, every day). It is shown with a to-confirm pill in the contact card and FAQ. Confirm or correct.
  • Credentials wording. “Master electrician”, “licensed, WCB, insured” and “fully licensed and insured” come from your Kijiji and YellowPages listings. The City of Calgary contractor list shows the business under Evanston but not the licence status or expiry. Confirm the master electrician certificate, contractor licence, WCB account and insurance are current before launch.
  • Years in business. BBB gives a start date of August 21, 2003 and the Kijiji ad says 23 years — consistent, and used in the hero and FAQ. The Yelp text says only “for the last several years”. Confirm 2003 is the year to build the story around.
  • Service list. Taken from YellowPages (renovations, new construction, basement development, wiring and rewiring, switch installation, service upgrades, landscape lighting, knob and tube removal) and Yelp (inspection, installation or replacement, repair). The YellowPages text was cut off after “knob and tube removal” — tell us what else belongs on the list.
  • Not claimed: HVAC and solar. Yelp files the business under heating and air conditioning too, and a search snippet from the old site mentions solar panels. Neither is confirmed anywhere else, so neither appears on this page. Say the word if either is real.
  • Service area. Confirmed: Evanston (City list), the T3P postal area (Sage Hill / Nolan Hill), and “Calgary and area”. Search snippets mention Airdrie, High River and Red Deer, but that text was not readable in full — it sits in a dashed to-confirm chip. The dashed map outlines are placeholders; name the communities you want listed.
  • Pricing, payment, guarantee wording. No prices are published. The hero, process and FAQ quote your Kijiji ad — “Reasonable price, quality and safety guaranteed, pay after 100% satisfaction” — word for word and attributed. A snippet also mentions work being “guaranteed and warrantied”, which was not readable in full and is not used. Decide whether that wording stays, and whether a deposit, payment methods or a warranty should be spelled out.
  • Reviews. BBB, Yelp and YellowPages all show zero reviews, so no stars, counts or quotes appear — only links to the listings and the BBB “0 complaints in 3 years” figure. With customer permission, real quotes can replace the links. A Google Business Profile would help most; none was found.
  • Project photos. None available publicly. Three before-and-after pairs are laid out and ready for phone photos. The hero artwork is an illustration, not a photo of your work.
  • Name and team. The page uses “Jack” because your own ad title does; the surname is left off until you say otherwise. Team size is not stated anywhere, so the site says “ask for Jack” and nothing more.
  • How a job goes. Steps 2 and 3 carry pills: free quote or site visit, written estimate, permits and inspection, scheduling, warranty. Dictate the real flow and the pills come off.
  • Listings to tidy. Your Yelp page is unclaimed and no Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram or HomeStars page was found. Claiming Yelp and adding a Google profile would give this site somewhere to send review traffic.
  • Quote form. The draft form has no backend. The live site needs a delivery route (a form service or an SMS relay) so requests really do reach your phone.
  • Draft banner. Remove the copper banner and the footer draft line when the site goes live.
