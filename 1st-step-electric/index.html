What 1st Step Electric does

From the panel to the last plug

Residential electrical, start to finish: the service coming into the house, the panel that distributes it, and every circuit, fixture and charger it feeds.

Panels

Panel changes & service upgrades

60, 100 and 200 amp residential panels and service upgrades. A modern 200 amp panel gives an older home the capacity for today’s loads — an EV charger, a hot tub, a kitchen full of appliances.

Wiring

Wiring & rewiring

New houses and old ones. Rewiring brings aging or undersized circuits up to the current code, with proper grounding and enough circuits for how you actually live in the house.

Repairs

Breakers & troubleshooting

Worn-out breakers changed out. Tripping circuits, dead outlets and flickering lights traced to the cause instead of the symptom.

Inspections

Inspections with a letter for your insurer

A walk-through of the electrical system with a written letter for your insurance company — the kind of letter insurers often ask for on an older home or at renewal.

Safety

Smoke & CO detector replacement

Detectors have a service life. Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms replaced, hard-wired or battery, to suit the house.

Lighting

Light & fan installations

New fixtures and ceiling fans hung, wired and switched.

EV

Electric car chargers

Level 2 home charging on its own dedicated circuit, sized to the charger and to what your panel can carry. Often the conversation that leads to a panel upgrade.

Permits

Electrical permits & inspections

Permits pulled and inspections arranged, so the work goes on record and the paperwork is done before the cover goes back on.

Upgrades

Pot lights, plugs & switches

Pot lights laid out and installed, plugs and switches added, fixtures swapped, ballasts replaced. Older work brought up to the current Canadian Electrical Code.

Anything else

No job too big or too small.

Before this goes live: things to confirm with the owner
  • Who to ask for. One Kijiji ad signs off “Call Kirk (Master Electrician)”, the other “Call Lars (Master Electrician)”; BBB lists the owner as Kirk. The site says “ask for Kirk” everywhere and uses no last name. Confirm the name, and whether Lars should appear too. Pills sit on the contact card and in the footer.
  • BBB “A+” claim. The Kijiji ads say “BBB Business rating with an A+”, but the BBB profile itself shows Not Accredited, Not Rated, 0 reviews. The site does not repeat the A+ anywhere — it shows only what the profile shows (founded 2008, 0 complaints in 3 years). If an A+ badge matters, that is a separate conversation with the BBB; the ads should be corrected either way.
  • Hours. Three versions in public: “On call all the time. 24Hours- 7 days a week!” (Kijiji), 8:00 AM to 10:00 PM daily (Yelp snippet) and “Open 24 hours” (a search-engine card). The contact card, FAQ and footer hold a to-confirm pill until one is chosen.
  • Years. The BBB profile and the Alberta registry both say founded / incorporated July 8, 2008 (18 years in 2026). An ad title says “Master Electrician 25 yrs exp” (the electrician, not the company) and directory snippets say “licensed and insured for over 20 years”. The page uses 2008 only. The registry also shows the corporation was named “White Lightning Inc” until August 2011 — confirm whether it traded under that name and whether to mention it. A pill sits in the FAQ.
  • Reviews and ratings. No star figures are shown except the Kijiji seller rating (5.0, no count displayed), which was seen directly and is attributed. Third-party figures seen only in search snippets — TrustedPros 2.4 from 4 reviews, a Google figure of 2.8 from 32 via a third-party document, a Yelp figure that could not be loaded — are unverified and not shown. Only the BBB profile is linked now; a to-confirm pill sits in the reviews row. Decide which platforms to link (Yelp, Google, TrustedPros) and whether to claim a Google Business Profile.
  • Email, social media, booking. None found anywhere public. Email slots on the contact card and footer carry a pill; no social links are shown. Supply an address you want published, if any.
  • Pricing. No prices are published anywhere. “Lowest rates in Calgary” and “free quotes on site” appear only in a directory snippet that could not be loaded, so neither is used. Add prices or rate ranges only if you want them on the site.
  • Service area. Only “Calgary and the surrounding area” is stated by the business; Signal Hill comes from directory listings. The BBB listing shows only a mailing address, which is not treated as a location. The dashed outlines on the map are placeholders — name the communities you want listed, or the whole city.
  • Address. There is no storefront. The addresses on Kijiji, Yelp and TrustedPros appear to be homes, the BBB listing is a mailing address and the registry address is a shared registered office — none is shown, on purpose. The site says “SW Calgary” only.
  • Project photos. None available publicly. Three before-and-after pairs are laid out as designed placeholders; the hero and diagram are illustrations and say so.
  • How a call goes. Steps 2 and 3 carry pills: site visit or phone quote, written quotes, what “same-day service” means in practice, scheduling, deposit, payment. Dictate the real flow and the pills come off.
  • Warranty and credentials. “100% Guaranty with 5 year Warranty”, “licensed and insured” and “Master Electrician” are all taken from the Kijiji ads. Confirm what the warranty covers (pills in the why band and FAQ), confirm the line in the why band about a Master Electrician being needed to pull electrical permits in Alberta (pill there too), and have the licence, insurance certificate and Master Electrician certificate to hand before launch.
  • Directory-only services. Pot lights, added plugs and switches, ballast replacement and bringing work up to the current Canadian Electrical Code come from a YellowPages / Yelp description seen only as a search snippet. Confirm they belong on the list.
  • Kijiji ads. Both ads were live on September 22, 2026; their posting dates are not visible. Once the site is up, point the ads at it, fix the A+ line and settle the Kirk / Lars sign-off so every listing says the same thing.
  • Quote form. The draft form has no backend. The live site needs a delivery route (a form service or an SMS relay) so requests really do reach your phone — and a domain: 1ststepelectric.ca did not resolve to any website when checked for this draft.
  • Legal name and draft banner. The registry spells it “1ST Step Electric Ltd”; the site uses “1st Step Electric Ltd.” — say if you prefer otherwise. Remove the yellow draft banner and the footer draft line when the site goes live.
