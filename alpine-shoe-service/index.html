Why repair

Repair the pair you have, not replace it

A few reasons a cobbler’s bench still earns its place on a high street.

  • Fit you cannot buyA broken-in upper has already shaped itself to your foot. A resole keeps that and replaces only what has worn out.
  • Usually the cheaper routeNew heels or a new sole cost a fraction of a comparable new pair of well-made shoes, and the shoes come back better than “good enough”.
  • Less in the landfillShoes are hard to recycle. Keeping a pair in service for years longer is the greenest thing that can happen to them.
  • A shop you can walk intoA real storefront on Kensington Road NW, in Hillhurst. Hand the shoes over in person and talk about what they need.

Most good leather shoes and boots can be. Welted and Blake-stitched shoes are built to be resoled; cemented (glued) soles can often be replaced too, and many sneakers with a separate sole unit as well. What matters is the condition of the upper — if the leather is sound, the sole is the easy part. A cobbler can tell by looking, which is why the answer is always to bring them in.

If the shoes fit well and the upper is in decent shape, repair is usually the better value: you keep a pair that is already broken in and pay a fraction of what an equivalent new pair costs. For cheaply made shoes with a moulded sole and a thin synthetic upper, replacement sometimes makes more sense. Ask at the counter; a good repair shop will tell you when a job is not worth doing.
