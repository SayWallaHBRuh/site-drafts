To confirm with the owner: are walk-ins welcome?

Monday to Friday 10:00 AM – 7:00 PM, Saturday 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM and Sunday 11:00 AM – 5:00 PM, as listed on Fresha. Holiday hours may differ — call ahead on a long weekend.

Gel is a polish that cures hard under an LED lamp; it keeps a glossy finish for weeks and is the usual choice on natural nails. Acrylic is a sculpting material — a liquid and powder that hardens in the air — used to add length and strength as a full set. Both are on the menu here, along with extensions in either.

A “dry” manicure: instead of soaking, the technician uses an electric file with fine bits to lift and remove the cuticle very precisely, so polish can be applied right up to the skin. The result looks cleaner for longer as it grows out. It is listed on the salon’s Fresha page.

Draft notes

Before this goes live: things to confirm with the owner

Everything on this page comes from the salon’s public listings. Nothing has been invented — where a fact could not be verified it is left as a dashed slot. Here is the full list of what needs a yes from the owner.

  • Another agency may be pitching you. No website owned by the salon was found, but a template page carrying your name, address, phone and hours exists on a marketing agency’s subdomain. Its “About” text describes a salon in Annapolis, Maryland, and every link is a placeholder — it looks like a spec demo, not your site. Worth knowing before you talk to anyone.
  • Prices. None are published anywhere. Every menu item shows a dashed “Price to confirm” slot and the FAQ answer is left open. Send the price list and they drop straight in.
  • Hours. Taken from the Fresha listing: Mon–Fri 10–7, Sat 10–5, Sun 11–5. Directory snippets agree on Mon–Sat; the Sunday closing time appears only on Fresha. Confirm, and add holiday hours.
  • “Established in 2016”. This appears only in a review-aggregator search snippet whose source is unknown, so the page does not say it. If it is right, a “since 2016” line can go in the hero and footer.
  • Reviews and ratings. Yelp shows 18 reviews in its page title (the page itself was blocked); an aggregator snippet quotes Google 4.40/5 from 84 reviews and Facebook 4.52/5 from 18; one obscure listing site shows 4.4/5 from 48 ratings. None could be verified at the source, so no stars or counts appear on the page — only links to Yelp and Facebook. With permission, real client quotes can replace the links.
  • Email. No business email was found (the one on your 2024 job posting was redacted). None is shown; add the one you want on the site.
  • Russian manicure. Listed on Fresha only. Confirm it is still offered; otherwise it comes off the menu, the gallery and the FAQ.
  • Waxing and solar nails. Both appear only in directory search snippets, so they are tagged “To confirm” on the menu. Say yes or no and the tags go.
  • Online booking. Your Fresha page is a directory entry — Fresha states the salon is not affiliated with it and tells visitors to call. If you want online booking, pick a tool and the “Book” button changes to it.
  • Walk-ins, deposits, payment, cancellations. Not stated anywhere public. Steps 1 and 3 and the first FAQ are flagged; dictate the real policy and the pills come off.
  • Cleanliness and sterilisation. Nothing is published about your hygiene routine, so the page makes no claim. Tell us what you do (autoclave, single-use files, liners) and it becomes one of the strongest lines on the site.
  • Accessibility. Directories list wheelchair-accessible parking, entrance and washroom. These are directory attributes, not your own statement — confirm before they are stated plainly.
  • Parking and transit. Neither is published. Tell us where clients park and the nearest transit stop, and both go into the Visit section.
  • Hiring. A September 2024 posting sought two full-time nail technicians (now closed). If you are still hiring, a “Join the team” line can be added.
  • Instagram. No account was found for the salon; searches returned unrelated shops. If one exists, it belongs in the footer next to Facebook. The Facebook page is login-walled — the like and check-in counts seen in snippets are not shown.
  • Photos. None are available publicly. The hero is an illustration and the gallery holds six swatch placeholders, ready for your own photos.
  • Names. No owner or staff names are published, so none appear. Two review-style phrases found in search snippets read like client reviews rather than your own words, so they were not used as copy.
  • Legal name. “Hawaiian Nails & Spa Ltd.” is used as it appears on Fresha, Facebook and your job posting. Confirm it matches the registered name for the footer.
  • Call-back form. The draft form has no backend. The live site needs a delivery route (a form service or an SMS relay) so requests really reach the desk.
  • Draft banner. Remove the coral banner at the top and the draft line in the footer when the site goes live.
