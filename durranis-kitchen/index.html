Draft notes

Before this goes live: things to confirm with the owner
  • Owner’s name. Never shown publicly, so the site says “the kitchen”. Add a name and a line about who cooks if you’d like to be introduced.
  • Cuisine label. Adeel recalls Pakistani/Afghan home cooking, but the page never says so. The site says “home-style” and quotes your own words; confirm any regional label before it appears.
  • Delivery zones and prices. The post says only “depends on location”. A short list of areas and rates fills the how-it-works step, the FAQ and the order card.
  • Pickup. Not mentioned anywhere. Yes or no, and where, if yes. Only “Calgary” is shown — no address will appear on the site.
  • WhatsApp. Not published. If you take orders there, it becomes a live button in the hero, the order card and the footer.
  • Catering. Only the intro’s “catering service” is public. Minimum order, lead time and what a catering menu looks like — the three pills on the page come off once you say.
  • Always five dishes, always Saturday? Only the 26 September week is known, so every number on the page is labelled “example week”. Confirm the pattern and the labels can relax.
  • Hours. Not published. Add them to the order card, FAQ and footer.
  • Permit and inspections. “AHS approved” and “Alberta Health Approved” appear on the page only as your own statements, with your offer to share the permit on request. A Calgary inspection index (kitchenchecked.com) lists a “Durrani’s Kitchen” with 2 recorded inspections in a search snippet, but the record itself could not be opened, so nothing about it is stated on the page. If the permit can be shown, a number or scan could sit in Good to know.
  • Photos and captions. The three photos come from your public Facebook post. Please confirm they are yours, and confirm the captions — especially “Boneless Chicken Shimla Mirch”, which is a best guess. Three placeholder tiles are waiting for more.
  • Facebook following. The public page embed showed 1,121 followers on 22 September 2026. It isn’t on the page; it could appear as a small “join 1,100+ followers” line if you want it. The page category currently reads “Digital creator” — a food category would suit it better.
  • Menu alerts — the pitch. Because the menu changes weekly, customers can sign up once and get each week’s poster by text or email the moment it’s posted, in time to order by Wednesday. The draft form stores nothing; the live version needs a small subscriber list plus a text/email sender, with clear consent wording as Canada’s anti-spam rules require.
  • The weekly poster. The example-week section is where each week’s live menu goes. Decide how it gets there: you upload the poster, or the site picks up your Facebook post.
  • Order form. No backend in the draft. The live site needs a delivery route (a form service or text relay) so enquiries really reach your phone. No email is published anywhere, so none is shown.
  • Draft banner. Remove the gold banner and the footer draft line when the site goes live.
